# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-08-15

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** SOOCLY helps photographers find a visual Look that is calibrated for their exact camera or phone, see credible before/after proof, apply the right settings, and shoot with less post-processing.

**What it does:** SOOCLY organizes visual Looks separately from device-specific implementations. A user chooses their device, discovers a Look they like, reviews real sample photos and Default ↔ Look comparisons, then applies the exact settings for that device. Over time users can save Looks, bind gear, contribute real photos, follow creators, and return when new relevant Looks become available.

**Product category:** Camera look / film recipe discovery platform.

**Product type:** Consumer web platform with creator/community and digital-product potential.

**Business model:** Validation first. Initial Alpha is free. Future options include premium Look packs, creator revenue share, subscriptions, premium discovery/recommendation, and paid creator content. No monetization model is considered final yet.

## Target Audience

**Primary audience:** Enthusiast and creator photographers who use cameras/phones with meaningful in-camera color or image-style controls and care about attractive straight-out-of-camera results.

**Initial device audiences:**
- Fujifilm X100VI owners.
- Ricoh GR IV owners.

**Expansion audiences:** Other Fujifilm/Ricoh models, Sony users, iPhone users, and other cameras/phones where device-specific settings can materially change output.

**Primary use case:** "I know the kind of photo I want, but I do not want to spend time guessing which settings/recipe will get my device close to that result."

**Jobs to be done:**
- Find a good-looking style for the camera I already own.
- Know whether a Look works well for my specific device and shooting conditions.
- Apply proven settings without learning every technical control first.
- Reduce post-processing when possible.
- Save and revisit Looks I want to try.
- Discover what other photographers are producing with the same device/Look.

**Common scenarios:**
- A user sees a photo/reel and asks, "How do I get this look?"
- A new camera owner wants a small set of reliable Looks instead of hundreds of recipes.
- A photographer is going out to shoot and wants a Look appropriate for the device and context.
- A creator wants to package and distribute their visual style to followers.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Camera users can find many recipes/settings online, but discovery is fragmented and it is difficult to know which Look fits their device, whether the result is credible, and when/how to use it.

**Why current solutions fall short:**
- Many databases are heavily Fujifilm-centric.
- Huge recipe libraries create choice overload.
- Settings are often presented without enough context about conditions or use cases.
- The same settings can render differently on different camera models/generations.
- Beautiful sample photos may be shown without a controlled Default ↔ Look comparison.
- Social posts disappear quickly and are difficult to search or revisit.
- Users frequently still need to translate a desired visual result into technical settings themselves.

**What it costs users:** Time spent searching, testing, editing, switching recipes, and taking photos that do not match the expected look.

**Emotional tension:** "I bought this camera because I love the photos people make with it, but mine still look ordinary. What are they doing differently?"

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct competitors:**
- FilmSimRecipes — recipe discovery/community and productized Fujifilm recipes.
- film.recipes — large Fujifilm recipe library with app-oriented discovery.
- FUJISTYLE — Fujifilm recipe/app workflow including camera import and sharing utilities.
- Fuji X Weekly — established Fujifilm recipe/content ecosystem and strong category brand.

**Secondary competitors:**
- Ricoh-specific recipe sites/apps.
- Individual creator Gumroad/Etsy recipe packs.
- Social creators sharing settings directly in posts/videos.

**Indirect alternatives:**
- Lightroom presets and manual post-processing.
- Users building their own in-camera profiles/settings.
- Free recipes scattered across Reddit, YouTube, blogs, Pinterest, Instagram, and camera forums.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**
- Look-first rather than parameter-first discovery.
- Cross-device architecture instead of one-camera-brand identity.
- Device-calibrated variants under one shared Look entity.
- Real visual proof, including Default ↔ Look comparisons.
- Gear-aware discovery and reactivation: users bind devices and receive relevant future Look updates.
- Creator ownership and community samples can turn each Look into a durable content/distribution asset.
- Product language can adapt to each ecosystem while the SOOCLY concept remains unified.

**How we do it differently:** SOOCLY starts from the visual outcome a user wants, then maps that Look to the correct device-specific implementation and evidence.

**Why that's better:** Users make fewer technical decisions, gain confidence before applying settings, and can participate in a growing body of proof for the exact gear they use.

**Why customers may choose us:** "Show me how this Look actually behaves on my camera, not just a list of settings somebody copied."

## Objections & Anti-Personas

**Objection 1: "There are already thousands of free recipes."**
Response: SOOCLY's value is not scarcity of parameter numbers. It is finding the right Look, verified device-specific implementation, proof, usage context, organization, and participation.

**Objection 2: "A recipe cannot guarantee the same result in every situation."**
Response: Correct. SOOCLY should never promise deterministic output across light, subject, exposure, lens, firmware, or device. The product should show conditions, shooting notes, comparison methodology, and real community results.

**Objection 3: "Why not just edit in Lightroom?"**
Response: Lightroom remains useful. SOOCLY is primarily for users who value a strong in-camera starting point or SOOC workflow and want to spend less time grading/editing.

**Not a good fit:**
- Users who only want generic AI filters applied after capture.
- Users who do not care about photographic style or device-specific output.
- Creators who want fabricated sample imagery presented as real device proof.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** Too many scattered recipes, inconsistent results, too much editing, difficulty remembering which settings work, and uncertainty about device compatibility.

**Pull:** Immediate visual proof, device-specific settings, simpler discovery, saved gear/Looks, real community shots, and trusted creators.

**Habit:** Users already browse free blogs, Reddit, YouTube, saved Instagram posts, or existing recipe apps.

**Anxiety:** "Will this Look actually work on my camera?" "Are these samples real?" "Will I need to pay for settings that are available free somewhere else?"

## Customer Language

**Verbatim-style problem language to preserve in research/copy:**
- "How do I get this look?"
- "What recipe should I use?"
- "Best recipe for my X100VI"
- "What settings are you using?"
- "Can I get this look straight out of camera?"
- "Does this recipe work on my camera?"
- "Why does the same recipe look different on my camera?"

**Preferred product vocabulary:**
- Look
- Your camera / your gear
- Works with / calibrated for / tested on
- Default vs Look
- Sample shots
- Settings
- Best for / works best in
- Save / Try / Add to My Gear

**Use ecosystem terms naturally for discovery/SEO:**
- Fujifilm / Ricoh: recipe, film simulation recipe.
- iPhone: Photographic Styles, camera settings.
- Sony: Picture Profiles / film looks / settings depending on actual device workflow.

**Avoid:**
- Claiming one universal recipe works identically across devices.
- Promising guaranteed professional photos.
- Calling AI-generated images real camera samples.
- Overusing technical jargon before showing the visual outcome.

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Visual, confident, concise, curious, credible.

**Communication style:** Show first, explain second. Use plain photography language. Technical detail belongs where it helps users reproduce the result.

**Brand personality:** Visual, practical, tasteful, trustworthy, creator-friendly.

## Proof Points

**Current proof:**
- Multiple established overseas products and communities already serve Fujifilm/Ricoh recipe demand.
- Search research has shown clear demand for core terms such as Fujifilm recipes, X100VI recipes, Ricoh recipes, and iPhone Photographic Styles, while many highly specific scene combinations have little/no measurable volume.
- Competitor organic-search research suggests generic recipe demand exists but is not large enough to rely on SEO alone; creator/social distribution should be a primary acquisition engine.

**Still needed:**
- SOOCLY user activation data.
- Creator acquisition/conversion data.
- Real Look performance and repeat-use data.
- Paid conversion/LTV data.

## Goals

**Primary business goal:** Validate a repeatable acquisition + activation + retention loop before building a large recipe catalog or complex monetization system.

**Primary user activation:** **Activated Gear Owner** — a user who binds at least one device and saves or tries at least one Look.

**Key Alpha actions:**
- Select/bind a device.
- View a Look.
- Interact with Default ↔ Look comparison.
- Save or Try a Look.

**Growth loop:** Creator/social content → Look page → device selection/binding → use/save → community contribution/share → new users → search/brand demand → device-relevant return.

## Product Stage Boundaries

**Alpha:** Browse Looks, device selection/binding, Look detail, Default ↔ Look, exact device settings, Save.

**V1 Growth:** Creator profiles/submission, Community Shots, share cards, Notify Me, creator/Look follows.

**V2 Intelligence:** Find My Look / reference-image matching, richer personalized recommendations, advanced discovery, broader device ecosystems.
